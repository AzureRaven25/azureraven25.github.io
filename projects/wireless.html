I worked out the wireless protocol behind a set of commercial accessibility door buttons, using nothing but the radio signals they send.

Why I looked

The push-to-open accessibility buttons on several campus buildings have no wire running to the door. I wanted to know how they talk to it.

So I set out to understand the protocol purely from the radio signals the buttons send. With open-source RF tools and a software-defined radio I captured and studied the transmissions, and worked out the key properties of the protocol with no datasheet or source code to start from.

I treated it strictly as a signal-analysis exercise and stopped once I had characterized how the protocol worked, without transmitting, replaying or interacting with any receiver.

How I did it

  1. Capture

    Recorded the buttons’ over-the-air transmissions with a software-defined radio.

  2. Find the channels

    Opened the captures in Universal Radio Hacker to find which frequencies were active and how the signal moved across them.

  3. Demodulate

    Identified the modulation as frequency shift keying and mapped the pattern it hops through across three channels.

  4. Read the structure

    Worked out the point-to-multipoint topology, one button to the door receivers, and the general structure of the data being sent.

What I found

Protocol
BEA’s proprietary wireless door and access activation protocol
Band
The 900 MHz ISM band, roughly 908 to 918 MHz
Frequency use
Hops between 3 channels
Modulation
Frequency shift keying (FSK)
Topology
Point to multipoint, from one transmitter to the receivers

Where I drew the line

I kept this project to passive analysis and characterization only. I did not transmit, replay or try to trigger any device, and this write-up leaves out anything that would help someone do that. The goal was to learn how a real wireless system works, not to interfere with one.

What it taught me

RF and wireless signal analysis, reverse engineering a protocol from first principles, working with a software-defined radio, and working from observation alone when there is nothing written down.
