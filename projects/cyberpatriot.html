The competition

CyberPatriot is the National Youth Cyber Education Program’s main competition. Teams of students race to find and fix security problems in operating system images under a strict time limit.

As cybersecurity head and chief of competitions for the GSMST computer science club, I led our team to three straight National Semifinals at the Platinum tier, the highest regular tier before the national finals.

How a round works

Each round hands the team virtual machines running a mix of Ubuntu, Fedora, Windows 10 and Windows Server, each one broken on purpose with real-world problems. Unauthorized users, weak passwords, services that should be off, firewall mistakes, malware that hides itself and policy violations. Points come in live as you find and fix each one before time runs out.

Staying at Platinum takes strong scores in every qualifying round. Reaching the National Semifinals three years running meant the team performed well all season, not in one lucky round.

What I did on the machines

Linux
User audits, service management, UFW and iptables firewalls, SSH hardening and file permission fixes on Ubuntu, Fedora and Debian
Windows
Local Security Policy, Group Policy, user account control, service audits and registry hardening on Windows 10 and Windows Server
Triage
Finding and ranking vulnerabilities under time pressure
Automation
Bash and PowerShell scripts for fast, repeatable fixes
Networks
Cisco Packet Tracer and Wireshark
Forensics
Log analysis and artifact recovery for the forensics questions

Teaching the club

Beyond competing, I ran the club’s weekly security sessions for more than 30 students. I wrote the lessons on finding, exploiting and fixing vulnerabilities, led hands-on labs, and coached members through practice rounds to get ready for each competition.
