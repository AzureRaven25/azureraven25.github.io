Reading a door button’s radio

I worked out the wireless protocol behind a set of commercial accessibility door buttons, using nothing but the radio signals they send.

Why I looked

The push-to-open buttons on several campus buildings have no wire running to the door. I wanted to know how they talk to it.

So I treated it as a pure signal-analysis problem. I captured what the buttons send, worked out the protocol from those captures, and stopped once I understood how it worked.

How I did it

  1. Step 1

    Capture

    Recorded the buttons' transmissions over the air with a software-defined radio.

  2. Step 2

    Find the channels

    Opened the captures in Universal Radio Hacker to find which frequencies were active and how the signal moved between them.

  3. Step 3

    Demodulate

    Identified the modulation as frequency shift keying and mapped the pattern it hops through across three channels.

  4. Step 4

    Read the structure

    Worked out that one transmitter talks to many receivers, and the general layout of the data it sends.

What I found

Protocol
BEA’s proprietary wireless door and access activation protocol
Band
The 900 MHz ISM band, roughly 908 to 918 MHz
Frequency use
Hops between 3 channels
Modulation
Frequency shift keying (FSK)
Topology
Point to multipoint, from one button to the door receivers

Where I drew the line

This was listening only. I never transmitted, replayed or tried to trigger any device, and this write-up leaves out anything that would help someone do that. The point was to learn how a real wireless system works, not to interfere with one.

What it taught me is RF signal analysis, reverse engineering a protocol from first principles, and working from observation when there is nothing written down.
