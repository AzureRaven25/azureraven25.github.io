October 2026

Stage 3, a streaming app on three screens

In October I rebuilt the site to feel like a streaming service. Home is now rows picked from your ratings, and each one says why it was picked. Search takes plain sentences, so you can ask for a slow burn thriller set on a boat and get Dead Calm and All Is Lost.

Then I wrote two native TV apps. The Apple TV app is SwiftUI and the Fire TV app is Kotlin with Compose for TV. Together they are about 17,000 lines across 70 files. They share the website’s accounts, lists and progress, and both are built for a remote.

Other October additions include an episode rating heatmap, outside ratings from IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes and Metacritic, franchise watch order, a release calendar, close-game alerts for teams you follow, and Wrapped, a yearly recap printed as a movie ticket.

On the TV

Captured in the Apple TV simulator, October 2026.

The TV apps are not the website in a frame. Each one has its own player that starts several video servers at once and keeps whichever plays first. On one movie the fastest server started in 1.8 seconds and the slowest took 26, so racing them means you only wait for the fastest.

They recover from stalled servers, sync progress back to the site, draw their own subtitles, and add TV-only touches like a Top Shelf row on the Apple TV home screen, skip intro, a sleep timer and picture in picture for live games.

April 2026

What I fixed in the API

Before the final demo I went through the Express API the way an attacker would and closed what I found. All of it shipped in one pull request.

  • Login and signup were open to password guessing. I capped them at 10 tries per 15 minutes per address, and chat and forum posts at 30 a minute.
  • Profile links accepted javascript: and data: URLs, a stored XSS waiting to happen. Those schemes are now rejected, and usernames and emails are checked against strict patterns.
  • The avatar field took up to 3 MB of text and request bodies up to 5 MB. I cut them to 500 characters and 1 MB.
  • The server sent no security headers. Helmet now adds HSTS, frame and content-type protections.
  • Private lists and profiles sit behind Supabase row level security, so a bug in the app still cannot read someone else’s data. The admin panel is gated by a role check the database enforces.

One thing I would change. The rate limiter keeps its counts in memory, which does nothing on Vercel’s serverless functions. It turns itself off there and leans on Vercel’s own protection, but a shared store would make it real.

I also connected the April chatbot to the catalog with retrieval-augmented generation and looked at how prompts can be used to abuse a setup like that.

March to April 2026

Stage 1, the class project

This is the version we turned in, with its original screenshots and write-up.

Binge started as a social tracker where you build a profile around everything you watch and read. You could browse movies, TV shows and books, rate them, track your watchlist, and see what your friends were into.

It sat somewhere between Letterboxd and Reddit, part personal journal and part social network, with a discussion forum on top. People followed each other, saw what was trending, built ranked lists together with live voting, and watched things together in synchronized watch rooms. A Groq chatbot gave recommendations, and an auto-moderator kept the forum clean without anyone reviewing posts by hand.

My parts were the chatbot, the season and episode tracker, live TV, an in-browser book reader, and the security pass above.

What it could do

  • BrowsingMovies, TV shows and books with search, filters and detail windows. A live TV page with channels. Global search on Cmd+K across everything.
  • Personal trackingA watchlist with Plan to Watch, Watching and Watched. Season and episode progress for shows, chapter and page for books. Star ratings and scores.
  • Social graphProfiles with an avatar, bio, follower counts and a public watchlist. Follow and unfollow, a following feed, a trending page and notifications.
  • Shared listsPublic or private named lists, collaborators invited by username, up and down voting to find the group’s pick, and a public link to share.
  • ForumSub-forums with icons and rules, posts with flair, hashtags and markdown, nested comments with voting, spoiler covers and a report button.
  • Watch roomsSeveral people watching the same thing at the same time, with a live chat beside the player.
  • AIA Groq chatbot for recommendations, and moderation that blocked offensive posts before they were published.
  • Admin panelUser management with search, promote and ban, media request approvals, and usage, chat log and error analytics.

How it was built

Front end
React 19 with React Router 7 and hand-written CSS, no component library. It talked to Supabase directly and fell back to the Express API for anything that needed a secret or server-side work.
Back end
Express 5 for the Groq chat and moderation, forum actions, notifications, admin actions and watch rooms. Supabase handled Postgres, sign-in and row level security.
Data
Catalogs seeded from TMDB, Plex, the Internet Archive and Open Library with scraper scripts we wrote. In April that came to 78,357 movies, 21,446 shows and 29,788 books.
Hosting
The front end on Vercel and the Express server as its own service.

Decisions worth explaining

Moderation ran as middleware on the Express server. Every new post and comment went through Groq before it reached the database, so offensive content never appeared at all instead of being flagged afterward.

Row level security in Supabase meant private lists and user data stayed protected even if the app had a bug, and the admin pages were gated by a role check the database enforced.

The whole UI was our own CSS so we had full control of the dark look across more than 40 views.
