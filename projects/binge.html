October 2026

Stage 3, a streaming app on three screens

In October I rebuilt the site to feel like a streaming service. Home is now rows picked from your ratings, each one saying why it was picked. Search takes plain sentences, so you can ask for a slow burn thriller set on a boat and get Dead Calm and All Is Lost.

Then I wrote two native TV apps. The Apple TV app is SwiftUI and the Fire TV app is Kotlin with Compose for TV. Together they are about 17,000 lines across 70 files. They share the website’s accounts, lists and progress, and both are built for a remote.

On the TV

Captured in the Apple TV simulator, October 2026.

The TV apps are not the website in a frame. Each one has its own player that starts several video servers at once and keeps whichever plays first. On one movie the fastest server started in 1.8 seconds and the slowest took 26, so racing them means you only wait for the fastest. They recover from stalled servers, sync progress back to the site, and draw their own subtitles.

April 2026

What I fixed in the API

Before the final demo I went through the Express API the way an attacker would and closed what I found. All of it shipped in one pull request.

  • Login and signup were open to password guessing. I capped them at 10 tries per 15 minutes per address, and chat and forum posts at 30 a minute.
  • Profile links accepted javascript: and data: URLs, a stored XSS waiting to happen. Those schemes are now rejected, and usernames and emails are checked against strict patterns.
  • The avatar field took up to 3 MB of text and request bodies up to 5 MB. I cut them to 500 characters and 1 MB.
  • The server sent no security headers. Helmet now adds HSTS, frame and content-type protections.
  • Private lists and profiles sit behind Supabase row level security, so a bug in the app still cannot read someone else’s data.

One thing I would change. The rate limiter keeps its counts in memory, which does nothing on Vercel’s serverless functions. It turns itself off there and leans on Vercel’s own protection, but a shared store would make it real.

I also connected the April chatbot to the catalog with retrieval-augmented generation and looked at how prompts can be used to abuse a setup like that.

May to September 2026

Stage 2, phones and profiles

After the semester a few of us kept going. We rebuilt every page for phones with a bottom tab bar and pull to refresh, added household profiles with a kids filter, and wrote end-to-end tests in Playwright.

The team also cut the forum, the watch rooms and the first chatbot, and replaced the AI picks with a matching algorithm that runs on your own ratings. The site got smaller and faster.

March to April 2026

Stage 1, the class project

These are the screenshots from the version we turned in.

The first Binge sat somewhere between Letterboxd and Reddit. You rated movies, shows and books, tracked what you were watching, followed friends, built shared lists with voting, and argued in a forum. Watch rooms let people watch the same thing together with a chat on the side.

My parts were the Groq chatbot, the season and episode tracker, live TV, an in-browser book reader, and the security pass above. The UI was all hand-written CSS with no component library.
